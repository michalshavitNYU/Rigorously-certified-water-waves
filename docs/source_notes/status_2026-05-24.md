## 1. Headline

**The WaveCAP Lean proof is at its target trust footprint.** The two
giant trust axioms (`cert_residual_le`, `cert_D_ge`) that previously
asked the reader to trust the entire Julia residual computation are now
**derived theorems**, proved from:

  - Mathlib + Lean's three standard axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`,
    `Quot.sound`).
  - **7 atomic `cert_arb_*` axioms** — one per Arb-primitive operation
    used in the Julia pipeline (`trigpoly_to_taylor`, `ts_mul`,
    `ts_deriv`, `inv_series`, `fft_hilbert_series`, `dmin_bound`,
    `sup_bound_le_eps0`).

This is the trust trade the roadmap aimed for: trust each FLINT/Arblib
primitive separately instead of the entire residual algorithm as a black
box.

The Julia side has certified the PINN-trained Stokes wave with
`μ = λ²`, `β = β²` at `M_order=6, N_grid=100000, prec=128` to residual
upper bound **`2.30e-4`**.

## 4. What's left (if anything)

The project's headline trust-reduction goal is met. Remaining items are
polish / new science:

1. **Run a tighter / larger-N cert if needed for the paper.** The
   `N=131072` segfault diagnostic suggests memory pressure under threading;
   a single-threaded run or pre-allocated Arb pools would likely succeed
   if a sharper bound is wanted.
2. **Cleanup**: a few `linter.style.longLine` warnings in
   `Certificate.lean`; consider factoring the recurring
   `iteratedDerivWithin ↔ iteratedDeriv` adapter pattern.
3. **Verification**: independent rebuild from a fresh checkout to
   confirm reproducibility on a different machine.
4. **Long term**: backing the 7 atomic `cert_arb_*` axioms with a formal
   FLINT/Arblib correctness statement would eliminate even those (purely
   aspirational; FLINT is C and not formally verified).
